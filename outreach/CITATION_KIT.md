## What the release contains

The release covers **14 AI-video providers** and includes:

- provider pricing and billing scope;
- credits and allowances;
- plan entitlements;
- free-access states;
- commercial-use evidence status;
- relationship disclosures;
- source provenance and checked dates;
- documented material changes;
- machine-readable CSV and JSON distributions;
- methodology and data dictionary;
- release manifest and file-level SHA-256 checksums;
- reproducibility and validation scripts.

## Research controls

AI Video Signal uses the following controls:

- first-party evidence preferred;
- every material provider value is source/date-scoped;
- missing values are not silently interpreted as zero, no, unlimited or equivalent;
- historical states are preserved rather than overwritten;
- provider fact-checking can correct factual errors but does not grant editorial veto;
- commercial relationships are disclosed separately and do not affect evidence status;
- documentation review is not presented as hands-on model testing;
- non-comparable currencies or billing modes are not silently converted or normalized;
- CSV/JSON parity, evidence coverage and package checksums are validated automatically.

## Commercial Rights Evidence Status (CRES)

CRES is an evidence classification, not legal advice and not a provider-quality score.

States:

- `documented_clear`
- `documented_conditional`
- `unclear`
- `restricted`
- `not_checked`

## Approved Output Cost (AOC)

Methodological definition:

`AOC = nominal generation cost × expected attempts per approved output`

If attempts are not empirically measured, AOC must be presented as a scenario or assumption, not as an observed result.

## What this release does NOT claim

- It is not a hands-on visual-quality leaderboard.
- It does not claim that 14 providers represent the entire AI-video market.
- It does not convert non-comparable provider states into a single quality score.
- It does not treat affiliate relationships as evidence of provider quality.
- It does not claim global authority merely because a DOI exists.
